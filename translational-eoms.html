    Section 6.1 Translational Equations of Motion

    The translational equations of motion of satellites are fairly simple given that everything is written in the inertial frame. The position vector of the vehicle is \(\vec{r} = [x,y,z]^T\) and the velocity is \(\vec{V}_{B/I}=[\dot{x},\dot{y},\dot{z}]^T\text{.}\) The acceleration of the vehicle is found by summing the total forces on the body and dividing by the mass of the vehicle. In the equation below \(N_\text{Earth}\) is the number of planetary bodies acting on the vehicle while \(\vec{F}_P\) is the force imparted by thrusters.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \vec{a}_{B/I} = \frac{1}{m_s}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_\oplus}F_i + \vec{F}_P \right)\tag{6.1.1} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Note that for a spacecraft the magnitude of the gravitational acceleration vector is on the order of \(\pm 10~\text{m/s}^2\text{.}\) Sources point to solar radiation pressure being on the order of \(4.5~\mu\text{Pa}\) [37]. For a 1U CubeSat (10 cm x 10 cm) the force would be equal to \(0.45~\text{mN}\text{.}\) A 1U CubeSat has a nominal mass of 1 kg which would accelerate the CubeSat on the order of \(0.45~\text{mm/s}^2\text{,}\) which is considerably less than gravitational acceleration. Furthermore, using the standard aerodynamic drag equation (\(0.5\rho V^2 S C_D\)), where conservative estimates are used, the aerodynamic force at 600 km above the Earth’s surface would be about \(3.0~\text{nN}\) [38]. This assumes a density equal to \(1.03 \times 10^{-14}~\text{kg/m}^3\text{,}\) a velocity equal to \(7.56~\text{km/s}\text{,}\) and a drag coefficient equal to 1.0 [39]. A force this small would impart an acceleration of about \(3.0~\text{nm/s}^2\) which is also considerably less than gravitational acceleration. These forces cannot be neglected for longer missions but can be ignored where appropriate. For an aircraft and quadcopter the equations of motion are typically written in the body frame. As such the derivative transport theorem is used and the translational equations of motion are written as the following.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \begin{Bmatrix} \dot{u} \\ \dot{v} \\ \dot{w} \end{Bmatrix} = \frac{1}{m} \begin{Bmatrix} X\\Y\\Z\end{Bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -r & q \\ r & 0 & -p \\ -q & p & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} u\\v\\w\end{Bmatrix}\tag{6.1.2} \end{equation}
